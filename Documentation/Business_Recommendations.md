## 1. Fix the Outlet pricing model

Outlets hold 20.2% of sales but 7.7% of profit, with an average discount of 25% and a margin of 9.2% (vs 28.0% elsewhere).

**Action:** review the Outlet discount policy and assortment. Set a floor on the discount per product (based on unit cost) and move stock that needs 40–50% off through a clearance process instead of normal Outlet trading.

**Scenario:** raising Outlet margin from 9.2% to 20% on the same sales ($65.7M) adds about **$7.1M** profit. That still leaves Outlets well below the 28% earned by other formats.

**Open question:** are Outlets meant to be a clearance format? If so, track them against a clearance-specific target (sell-through, not margin).

## 2. Cap deep discounts (35%+)

The 14,647 orders at 40–50% discount, all of them in Outlet stores, lost **$2.2M** on $9.5M of sales, and discounts do not increase units per order (flat at about 2.1).

**Action:** require approval above a set discount level (for example 30%) and block discounts that take an item below unit cost.

**Scenario:** even if these orders were only brought to break-even, profit rises by $2.2M (+2.8%). The real figure depends on whether the volume would survive; test with a controlled pilot in a small group of stores before a full rollout.

## 3. Reduce dependence on Laptop Model 190

One product delivers 31.1% of sales at a 14.4% margin (company average without it: 28.6%).

**Action:**
- Negotiate unit cost or review list price. Each +1 pp of margin is worth about $1.0M.
- Set a stock-cover and supplier-continuity plan; a stock-out here hits almost a third of revenue.
- Check whether the low margin is a deliberate traffic-driver. If so, measure whether it brings basket-mates; if not, treat it as a pricing error.

**Needed data:** supplier terms and basket composition around this product (not in the dataset).

## 4. Review regional target allocation

North and East miss target in every month. Their targets are about **2.1× higher per store** than Central and South, while sales per store are similar everywhere. Allocating the same total target by store count would put every region at 128–134%.

**Action:** test whether regional targets should be built from store count, historical sales per store and local growth rather than a top-down split. Raise the company-wide level too: the business has never missed a monthly target, and targets grow slower (8.7–11.5%) than actual sales (11.4–17.1%).

**Caution:** review the target allocation before drawing conclusions about North and East execution; the dataset cannot rule out genuine regional differences.

## 5. Investigate Electronics returns

Electronics accounts for 70.7% of refunded value ($15.3M) and Laptop Model 190 alone for $7.7M. Return rates are 7.9–8.0% in Electronics and Home & Kitchen against about 4% in Furniture and Office Supplies.

**Action:** collect return reasons (defect, wrong item, change of mind, damaged in delivery) for Electronics, starting with laptops. Add **Net Sales** (sales minus refunds, $302.9M) as a headline metric so the report does not overstate revenue.

## 6. Treat Online growth as a finite engine

Online provides all net growth, but its growth rate is falling (+38% → +33% → +26%) while Retail (−6.9%) and Partners (−8.2%) shrink.

**Action:** model what happens when Online growth reaches the pace of overall demand. Decide deliberately whether to defend Retail and Partners (their margin is the same as Online's) or to reallocate their cost base.

## 7. Retain high-value accounts, restart acquisition

The top 10% of customers deliver 48.5% of sales; Corporate customers spend 2.3× a Consumer customer. Meanwhile first-time buyers fell from 2,701 to 108 a year and growth now relies on larger spend per existing customer (+35%).

**Action:** assign account management to the top decile and Corporate accounts, and set a minimum new-customer target. Track repeat purchase and churn once order-level customer history is available.

## 8. Plan around Q4

November and December together make up 22.4% of annual sales, and the pattern repeats every year.

**Action:** build inventory, staffing and fulfilment plans around Q4, and apply the discount rules above hardest in this period, when promotions are most intense.

## Decision-making principle

Use the dashboard to identify where the issue is, then use operational data (supplier terms, return reasons, store-level costs, customer history) to diagnose why before committing money to a fix.
